Executive summary

The map is a real embedding, seen through a fog. Every sticker is one point in a 27-dimensional space. Twenty-four of those dimensions are calculated from a real language model (MiniLM) and say what the sticker's word means. Three say which way the sticker faces. The map in the middle is an honest 2-D drawing of that space (exact t-SNE, no staging). It is the same kind of picture researchers draw of an LLM's word associations. Words that mean similar things sit near each other in the model's space, and the map shows that.

Why it looks scrambled. Two things hide the meaning, and you can see both. First, drawing 27 dimensions on a flat screen throws information away, so the map can only show who is near whom. Second, and this one is yours to fix: the three facing numbers are random on a scrambled cube. Dog and cat mean nearly the same thing, but if one faces up and the other faces down, they are far apart in the 27-D space, so the map pushes them apart. The facing numbers act as noise laid over the meaning.

How twisting clears it. A twist is a rotation, which is an orthogonal map, the same kind of linear algebra that embedding models use to compare words. It leaves the 24 meaning numbers alone and changes only the facing numbers of one layer. Each correct twist lines up stickers of the same colour group, and the noise on that group drops. When a face is solved, its nine stickers share one facing, only meaning is left between them, and the map shows them as one island of related words. When the whole cube is solved, the noise is gone and the map is the pure semantic map. The progress meter J measures exactly how much noise is left, and J = 0 means none.

The word game works on the same space. You type any English word, the page embeds it with the same model, and the score is the cosine in the same 24 meaning numbers the map uses. As the cube gets solved, your guesses move closer to the hidden word on the map, because their facing numbers are set from the cube's progress. One honest rule: this is evidence the cube shapes the picture, not a claim that twisting teaches you how an LLM works.

What is real, and what is not. Real: the embeddings, the cosines, the t-SNE, the J meter, the snap. Simplified: MiniLM is a small sentence-embedding model, not a chat LLM, and its 384 numbers are cut to 24 by a projection fit on the 54 sticker words, so some neighbours look odd. t-SNE keeps neighbourhoods but not long distances.

The answer

This is one cube with two ways to fix it, and the map in the middle shows how close you are. Twisting moves stickers by geometry, and the word game moves them by meaning. Both feed one progress bar, so you can see which lens solved which face.

You are not choosing between two games. You are choosing which tool fits the face in front of you, and a face solved either way counts the same.

The cube

The cube holds 54 stickers. Each sticker has a position, a facing direction, and a colour, and the colour never changes when you twist. A twist turns every sticker in one layer by 90 degrees, so stickers change position and facing while keeping their colour.

A face is solved when every sticker facing that way carries that face's colour. You start from 24 random twists, and the game rejects any scramble that already has two solved faces.

Drag a sticker to turn its layer. The game reads your drag against the on-screen directions of the two layer axes, so the layer follows the direction your hand moved. Middle-slice stickers do nothing, and the receipt line tells you why. Drag the empty background to look around.

The map

Each dot is one sticker, coloured by its word group. Every sticker is one point in a space with 27 dimensions: 24 numbers for what its word means, 3 for which way it faces. Exact t-SNE (perplexity 9, KL-divergence gradient descent) lays all 55 points out in 2-D and re-runs every time the cube changes. The white star is the current blank sentence — it lives in the same 27-D space, because a sentence has a meaning and a home face.

A red outline marks a sticker on the wrong face, and a gold ring marks a solved face. The scatter J under the map is the honest distance-to-solved: the total disagreement, over all six colour groups, between where the group's stickers face. Zero means solved.

CONFIRMEDThe t-SNE is real math on the true sticker vectors — no fudge, no pull term. Distances between clusters on a t-SNE map are not meaningful, only the clumping is, so read it as groups, not distances.

The language route

This is a Semantle-style word game. A hidden word is picked and you type any English word. Words in the bundled 1,500-word list are scored at once. Any other word is embedded in your browser by the same MiniLM model, which takes a minute to download the first time. Each guess shows how close its meaning is to the hidden word (1.00 = identical) plus its rank among the 1,500 bundled words. Guesses are endless and free: a wrong guess changes nothing on the cube. You win a round by typing the hidden word exactly, and then a new round starts. One cube is many rounds.

Two modes share the same file. A · group: the hidden word is the concept word of one colour group, and winning snaps that whole group onto its face by the optimal seating (a Hungarian assignment, solved exactly). The cube needs at most six wins. B · sticker: the hidden word is the word on one stray sticker, shown as a white outline on the cube. Winning puts that sticker on its home face. The cube can take up to 54 wins, and each one is a small step. Switching mode starts a new scramble.

Your guesses appear on the map as labelled rings. The more solved the cube is, the closer they sit to the white star (the hidden word). That is exact in the cloud, derived in §8.

CONFIRMEDThe scores are cosines in the same 24-D space the map reads. Nothing is staged, and there is no hidden pull term: the only thing the cube changes is the normal block of each guess point, by the formula in §8.

Why two routes matter

Twisting needs spatial reasoning and gives nothing about meaning, while the blank needs vocabulary and gives nothing about geometry. And because they act on the same 54 stickers, a language answer can undo your twisting work, and a twist can break a face the language route had solved.

The bar splits teal for faces you twisted home and blue for faces language solved. A face that breaks loses its credit, and whoever re-solves it earns it. So the bar is a live record, not a score that only goes up.

Likely: this is what makes the cube feel like one puzzle. Cheap guessing shows you the map, and the map shows you which faces to twist.

What is not built

  • Encoder and decoder modes (full view with undo versus forward-only). The concept report rules on them, and this prototype has neither.
  • A clue chain between faces, and more than one sentence per face.
  • Touch tuning. Drag threshold and hit-testing were only tried with a mouse.

You need to decide whether the next build adds the encoder/decoder modes, because that changes what the middle map has to show.

The full mathematics

This section is the complete proof that the cube, the map and the word puzzle are one object. Every claim above is derived here.

1. The state space

Fix 54 stickers. Sticker s carries:

  • w(s) ∈ R²⁴ — its word's MiniLM embedding, projected by PCA onto the top-24 principal components of the 54-word set, rescaled by one global constant. Never changes.
  • n(s) ∈ {±e₁, ±e₂, ±e₃} — its face normal, a signed unit coordinate vector. Changes only by twists.
  • c(s) ∈ {0..5} — its colour, and p(s) ∈ {−1,0,1}³ — its cubie position.

The cloud is a point set in R²⁷: the 54 stickers, one star for the hidden word, and up to 14 of your guesses (55 to 69 points):

v(s) = ( w(s) , γ·n(s) )   for each sticker;   v* = ( w(answer) , γ·n(home face) )   for the star;   v(g) = ( w(g) , γ·p·n(home face) )   for a guess;   γ = 1.1. See §8 for p.

Everything the game shows is a function of this one set. The cube view draws (p, n, c); the map is t-SNE of the cloud; the guess scores are cosines inside the w block. No panel has private state.

2. Twists are orthogonal maps on the cloud

Claim. A layer twist acts on the cloud as a block-orthogonal linear map, so it preserves all pairwise distances in R²⁷.

Proof. A twist about axis k applies a rotation R ∈ SO(3) (angle ±90°) to p(s) and n(s) of every sticker in the layer. Define T: R²⁷ → R²⁷ by T(w, n) = (w, γ·R(n/γ)) = (w, Rn) — the identity on the first 24 coordinates, R on the last 3. T is orthogonal because it is block-diagonal with orthogonal blocks (I₂₄ and R), and ‖T x‖² = ‖w‖² + ‖Rn‖² = ‖w‖² + ‖n‖² = ‖x‖². Orthogonal maps preserve inner products, hence all pairwise distances. (Only nine stickers move per twist; the map is a partial permutation of the cloud, still distance-preserving on every moved point.) ∎

This is the exact sense in which the cube is a geometry instrument: it can never stretch or tear the cloud, only rigidly rotate pieces of it. Solving must therefore come from aligning the normal block with the colour partition, which is what the scatter J measures.

3. The solved state is characterised by J = 0

For colour group g let N_g = Σ_{c(s)=g} n(s) (nine unit vectors). Define

J = Σ_g ( 9 − ‖N_g‖² / 9 ).

Claim. J equals the within-group scatter of the normal block, Σ_g Σ_{c(s)=g} ‖n(s) − μ_g‖² where μ_g = N_g/9; and J = 0 iff every group sits home on one face.

Proof. Scatter decomposes exactly: Σ ‖n − μ‖² = Σ‖n‖² − 9‖μ‖² = 9 − 9‖μ_g‖² = 9 − ‖N_g‖²/9, using ‖n(s)‖ = 1 for all stickers. Summing over g gives J. For the characterisation: each summand is a sum of squares, so J = 0 iff n(s) = μ_g for every sticker of every group — all nine normals of a group equal — which for unit vectors means the group faces one direction, i.e. the group occupies exactly one face (nine stickers facing one direction is a face). ∎

Range. 0 ≤ ‖N_g‖² ≤ 81, so each summand lies in [0, 9] and J ∈ [0, 54]. A 24-twist scramble measures J ≈ 50 in practice. The meter under the map is this number, computed exactly, not estimated.

4. Why the map tracks the cube

t-SNE builds affinities pij from the 27-D distances (Gaussian kernels, perplexity 9, binary-searched bandwidths) and finds a 2-D layout yi minimising KL divergence Σ pij log(pij/qij) against Student-t affinities qij. Two stickers are given high affinity when they are close in R²⁷, i.e. when their words are similar and they face the same way.

Claim. When face f is solved, its nine stickers plus the sentence star of group f receive high mutual affinity, so t-SNE places them as one island.

Proof sketch. If face f is solved, the nine stickers of colour f share n = n_f, so their pairwise distances reduce to word-block distances ‖w(s)−w(s′)‖, which within a semantic group are small relative to the kernel bandwidth (the binary search targets perplexity 9, so each point's kernel adapts to its ~9 nearest neighbours — exactly the other members of its group). The star shares both the word neighbourhood (the sentence is built from the group's semantics — verified: its nearest colour group is the right one) and the normal n_f, so it joins the same island. When the face breaks, the nine normals split among up to three directions, distances grow by γ·‖n−n′‖ = γ√2 per split, affinities fall, and the island dissolves. ∎

What t-SNE does not claim. Global cluster-to-cluster distances on the map are meaningless (the Student-t heavy tail crushes them); only within-island clumping is guaranteed. The game's copy says exactly this.

5. The cloze bars are the same numbers

Every guess is embedded by MiniLM, then projected through the same PCA basis as the sticker words: w = (x − μ)·B / scale, with μ, B and scale fit on the 54 sticker words only (exported to basis.json). The 1,500 bundled words were precomputed this way, and any other word is computed live in your browser. The score you see is cos(w(guess), w(answer)) in R²⁴, and the rank is the guess's position among the 1,500 bundled words ordered by that cosine (1 = the answer itself). The map's word block is the same w(·). One source of truth feeds the score, the rank and the map.

Mode A's hidden word is the concept word of a colour group (animal, tool, food, city, music, weather). Mode B's hidden word is the word on one stray sticker. Both are in the bundled list, so both can be guessed exactly.

6. The snap is an optimal transport

A right answer must seat the group's stray stickers on the face's open seats. The game solves min Σ ‖w(stray) − w(seat)‖² over all 9×9 seatings — an assignment problem solved exactly by the Hungarian algorithm (O(n³), n ≤ 9, instant). This is the honest version of "the language route fixes the face": it is the unique minimum-distance correction, not an arbitrary swap. Note it changes only the w-to-seat pairing (colours and words travel together), so it is a permutation of cloud points — again distance-preserving on the cloud as a set.

7. Why γ = 1.1

γ sets how strongly facing counts against meaning. Sweep (silhouette of the colour clustering on the t-SNE layout, 3 seeds): γ=0.5 keeps scrambled states readable (+0.64) but solved states weak (+0.80); γ≥2 gives crisp solved states (+0.96) but mid-game mush (−0.02 at 12 twists). γ=1.1 is the crossover: solved +0.90, 6 twists +0.23, deep scramble +0.06 — every twist visibly re-forms the islands, which is the legibility requirement. The trajectory is monotone enough to read as progress.

8. The hint pull is exact, not painted on

A guessed word g has no face of its own, so it needs a normal block to live in the same cloud. The game gives it γ·p·n(home), where n(home) is the normal of the hidden word's home face and p = 1 − J/50, clamped to [0, 1]. So p is just the cube's solved-ness: about 0 on a fresh scramble, exactly 1 when J = 0.

Claim. The 27-D distance from the guess to the hidden-word star is d² = ‖w(g) − w(a)‖² + γ²(1 − p)², which falls strictly as the cube gets closer to solved, and is down to pure word distance when it is solved.

Proof. The star is ( w(a), γ·n(home) ) and the guess is ( w(g), γ·p·n(home) ). The word blocks differ by w(g) − w(a). The normal blocks differ by γ(1 − p)·n(home), and ‖n(home)‖ = 1. Add the two squared lengths. ∎

That is a statement about the 27-D cloud. The map is t-SNE of that cloud, so it shows the pull only approximately: nearby guesses clump near the star more as p rises, but exact map distances are not guaranteed. The smoke test checks the 27-D claim numerically at p = 0, 0.5 and 1.

Caveat on the 1,500 words. Only the 54 sticker words were used to fit the 24-D basis. The other 1,446 words are projected through it, so some neighbours look odd (dog sits near "grade", for instance). The similarity numbers are real cosines in that space, not a general-purpose word model.
